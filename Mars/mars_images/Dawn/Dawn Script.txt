Dawn: The Blue-Beam Time Machine

What

Deep in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter lie two ancient worlds: Vesta and Ceres. In 2007, NASA launched a spacecraft called Dawn to explore both of them. It became the first spacecraft ever to orbit two different worlds!

Q: Wait, it visited two worlds? How did it travel so far without running out of fuel?

A: It used a special engine called an ion engine! Instead of powerful rocket blasts, it released tiny particles of charged xenon gas to create a gentle, continuous push through space.

Why

Scientists wanted to understand how our solar system formed billions of years ago. Vesta is a rocky asteroid, while Ceres is an icy dwarf planet. By studying these two ancient worlds, scientists hoped to discover how the building blocks of planets formed and changed over time.

How

Dawn used its solar-powered ion engines to travel through space and change its orbit. First, it spent 14 months studying Vesta. Then it continued its journey to Ceres, where it arrived in 2015. Using special cameras, mapping instruments and gravity measurements, Dawn studied the surfaces and interiors of both worlds.

Q: Did Dawn ever return to Earth to show us what it found?

A: No! It sent its pictures and scientific data back using radio signals. In 2018, it ran out of the fuel needed to control its direction, so it could no longer communicate with Earth. Today, it remains in orbit around Ceres!

Outcome

Dawn made some amazing discoveries! It found evidence of salty water beneath the surface of Ceres and discovered a giant mountain on Vesta, rising about 22 kilometres above the surrounding terrain. These discoveries helped scientists understand how water and rocky worlds developed in the early solar system. Most importantly, Dawn proved that ion engines could take spacecraft on long journeys between distant worlds!
